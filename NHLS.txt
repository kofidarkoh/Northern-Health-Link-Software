iiiSYNOPSIS
The project, “GUI for Northern Health Link Software,” aims to design a mobile-first
graphical user interface for a healthcare platform that connects rural clinics in Northern
Ghana with medical specialists based mainly in Tamale. Patients from many rural districts
such as Karaga and Gushegu, often travel more than four hours to access basic specialist
consultation and diagnostic services. This delay in healthcare access, contributes to
avoidable deaths, worsens health outcomes, and reduces economic productivity as patients
and caregivers spend significant time and money seeking care.
Northern Health Link platform proposes a practical digital solution that will enable rural
clinics to consult specialists through secure video calls, share patient information, and
receive expert medical guidance without requiring patients to travel long distances. The
platform will also support the delivery of laboratory results and prescribed medication
through a coordinated motorcycle fleet, making healthcare access faster and more reliable
for the underserved communities.
The GUI will be designed with simplicity, speed, and accessibility in mind. Since many
users may have limited digital experience or work in low-resource clinical settings, the
interface will prioritize clear navigation, readable layouts, patient registration, appointment
scheduling, video consultation, lab result tracking, prescription management, and delivery
status updates.
The expected outcome of the project is a user-friendly healthcare interface that improves
communication between rural clinics and specialists, reduces unnecessary travel, speeds up
diagnosis and treatment, and strengthens healthcare delivery across Northern Ghana.
Ultimately, the project aims to support a more equitable health system where location is no

5INTRODUCTION
61. Introduction
Access to timely healthcare stills remain a major concern in rural and underserved
communities, especially where specialist doctors, diagnostic facilities, and referral hospitals
are concentrated in urban centres. Universal health coverage requires that people receive
needed health services without excessive travel, delay, or financial hardship (World Health
Organization [WHO] & World Bank, 2023). However, rural and remote populations often
experience lower health workforce availability and weaker access to specialist care than
urban populations (WHO, 2021).
In Northern Ghana, the problem is more of a concern because many rural districts depend
on Tamale for specialist consultation and advanced diagnostic services. According to the
2021 Population and Housing Census, the Northern Region has a population of 2,310,939,
with districts such as Karaga and Gushegu recording large rural populations (Ghana
Statistical Service [GSS], 2021). Karaga has a population of 114,225, with about 73.9%
living in rural areas, while Gushegu Municipal has a population of 153,965, with about
76.4% living in rural areas (GSS, 2021). These figures show that a significant number of
people live far away from the main urban health service hub.
The project titled “GUI for Northern Health Link Software” seeks to respond to this
challenge by designing a mobile-first graphical user interface for a digital healthcare
platform. The platform will connect rural clinics to specialists through video consultation
and support the movement of lab results and medication through a motorcycle delivery fleet.
Digital health systems can improve health service delivery when they are properly designed
around local health system needs, resources, and workflows (WHO, 2019). Similarly,
computer-mediated medical consultation has been identified as a useful way to connect
underserved Ghanaian health workers with wider medical expertise (Luk, Ho & Aoki, 2008).
The proposed GUI will therefore concentrate on usability, simplicity, and accessibility for
rural clinic staff, specialists, dispatch riders, and health administrators. Key functions will
include patient registration, appointment scheduling, video consultation, lab result
management, prescription handling, and delivery tracking. By reducing unnecessary travel
and improving communication between rural clinics and specialists, Northern Health Link
7can contribute to faster diagnosis, improved treatment decisions, and better healthcare access
for rural communities in Northern Ghana.
1.1 Problem Statement
More than 70% of specialists in Northern Ghana provide their services in Tamale, leaving
rural districts such as Karaga and Gushegu with limited direct access to specialist
consultation. Patients in these districts often travel more than four hours to Tamale for basic
diagnostics or specialist review. This creates delays in diagnosis and treatment, increases
transport costs, and reduces productivity for patients and caregivers.
The problem is worsened even by the rural nature of many districts in the Northern Region.
For example, the majority of people in Karaga and Gushegu live in rural communities, where
access to higher-level healthcare is more difficult (GSS, 2021). WHO (2021) observes that
rural and remote communities often suffer from health worker shortages and maldistribution,
which means that some populations are left behind even when health workers are available
nationally. These access gaps can contribute to late treatment, poor health outcomes, and
avoidable mortality.
Although digital health interventions can support health system strengthening, they must be
designed to fit real clinical workflows and local infrastructure constraints (WHO, 2019). At
present, many rural clinics lack a unified, easy-to-use interface that allows them to request
specialist support, conduct video consultations, manage diagnostic feedback, track
prescriptions, and coordinate last-mile medication delivery. Therefore, there is a need for a
mobile-first GUI that enables rural clinics in Northern Ghana to connect efficiently with
specialists in Tamale while also supporting lab result and medication delivery.
Main Aim
To design and develop a mobile-first graphical user interface for Northern Health Link
Software that connects rural clinics in Northern Ghana to specialists through video
consultation and supports lab result, prescription, and medication delivery management.
8Objectives:
1. To identify the user requirements and workflow needs of rural clinic staff, specialists,
patients, and motorcycle delivery personnel.
2. To design a mobile-first GUI that supports patient registration, appointment
scheduling, video consultation, lab result management, prescription handling, and
delivery tracking.
3. To evaluate the usability, accessibility, and effectiveness of the GUI in improving
communication between rural clinics and specialists.

11SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION
123. Software Requirements Specification
The software requirements specification describes the functional and non-functional
requirements of the Northern Health Link Software.
3.1 Functional requirements
The functional requirements specification describes the services, operations, and features
that the Northern Health Link Software must provide to support remote healthcare delivery
in rural districts of Northern Ghana. The system will serve rural clinic staff, specialists,
laboratory personnel, motorcycle delivery riders, system administrators, and patients.
1. User Registration and Login
The system shall allow authorized users such as clinic staff, specialists, laboratory
officers, dispatch riders, and administrators to create accounts and log in securely.
2. Role-Based Access Control
The system shall provide different access levels based on user roles. For example,
clinic staff can register patients, specialists can review cases, and dispatch riders
can update delivery status.
3. Patient Registration
The system shall allow clinic staff to register patients by capturing details such as
name, age, gender, contact, district, medical history, and emergency contact.
4. Patient Record Management
The system shall allow authorized users to view, update, and retrieve patient
medical records when needed.
5. Appointment Scheduling
The system shall allow rural clinics to book video consultation appointments with
available specialists in Tamale.
6. Video Consultation
The system shall support real-time video communication between rural clinic staff,
patients, and specialists.
7. Specialist Consultation Notes
The system shall allow specialists to record diagnosis, recommendations, referrals,
and treatment instructions after consultation.
8. Laboratory Request Management
The system shall allow clinic staff or specialists to request laboratory tests for
patients.
9. Lab Result Upload and Viewing
The system shall allow laboratory officers to upload test results and allow
authorized clinic staff and specialists to view them.
1310. Prescription Management
The system shall allow specialists to prescribe medication digitally after
consultation or lab review.
11. Medication Delivery Request
The system shall allow clinic staff to request medication delivery through the
motorcycle fleet.
12. Delivery Tracking
The system shall allow dispatch riders to update delivery status, including pending,
picked up, in transit, delivered, or failed delivery.
13. Notifications and Alerts
The system shall send alerts for appointment reminders, lab result availability,
prescription updates, and delivery status changes.
14. Search and Filtering
The system shall allow users to search for patients, appointments, lab results,
prescriptions, and deliveries using relevant filters.
15. Dashboard and Reports
The system shall provide dashboards showing appointments, pending consultations,
lab results, prescriptions, deliveries, and system activity.
16. Administrative Management
The system shall allow administrators to manage users, clinics, specialists, delivery
riders, and system settings.
3.2 Non-functional requirements
1. Usability
The GUI shall be simple, mobile-first, and easy to use by clinic staff with limited
technical experience.
2. Performance
The system shall load key pages quickly and respond to user actions within a
reasonable time, even on low-bandwidth networks.
3. Reliability
The system shall operate consistently and minimize failures during important
activities such as video consultation and lab result submission.
4. Security
The system shall protect patient records through secure login, password protection,
role-based access, and encrypted data transmission.
5. Privacy
The system shall ensure that only authorized users can access sensitive patient
information.
6. Availability
The system shall be available whenever rural clinics need to request consultations,
access lab results, or track medication delivery.
7. Scalability
The system shall support future expansion to more clinics, districts, specialists, and
delivery riders.
148. Compatibility
The GUI shall work on common mobile phones, tablets, and desktop browsers used
by clinics and health workers.
9. Accessibility
The interface shall use readable text, clear buttons, simple icons, and good contrast
to support different users.
10. Maintainability
The system shall be designed in a way that allows developers to update features, fix
errors, and improve the platform easily.
11. Data Integrity
The system shall prevent loss, duplication, or unauthorized alteration of patient
records, lab results, prescriptions, and delivery details.
12. Backup and Recovery
The system shall support regular data backup and recovery in case of system failure
or accidental data loss.

21DESIGN
225. Design
This section describes the proposed front end and back end design for the Northern Health
Link Software. The design uses TypeScript on both the client and server side to reduce
programming inconsistency, improve maintainability, and allow shared validation models
where appropriate.
The system is designed as a mobile-first web application because rural clinic staff, dispatch
riders, and health workers may use phones, tablets, or desktop browsers. The back end
exposes secure APIs, applies role-based access control, stores structured healthcare data, and
coordinates video consultation, laboratory, prescription, notification, and delivery
workflows.
5.1 Front End Design
The front end was implemented as a responsive React application written in TypeScript.
React is suitable because the system has several repeated interface patterns such as forms,
patient cards, dashboards, status badges, tables, modal dialogs, and role-based navigation
menus.
5.1.1 Front End Design Principles
 Mobile-first layout with large buttons, simple icons, readable text, and forms that
work well on small screens.
 Role-based navigation so each user sees only the screens needed for their job.
 Clear status indicators for appointments, lab results, prescriptions, and delivery
progress.
 Offline-aware feedback such as loading states, retry messages, and saved form
validation errors.
 Accessible contrast, meaningful labels, and simple wording for clinic staff with
limited technical experience.

import { Request, Response, NextFunction } from "express";
import jwt from "jsonwebtoken";
export type Role = "CLINIC_STAFF" | "SPECIALIST" | "LAB_OFFICER" | "RIDER" | "ADMIN";
export interface AuthUser {
id: string;
role: Role;
clinicId?: string;
}
declare global {
namespace Express {
interface Request {
user?: AuthUser;
}
}
}
export function authenticateJwt(req: Request, res: Response, next: NextFunction) {
const header = req.headers.authorization;
const token = header?.startsWith("Bearer ") ? header.slice(7) : null;
if (!token) {
return res.status(401).json({ message: "Authentication token is required." });
}
try {
req.user = jwt.verify(token, process.env.JWT_SECRET!) as AuthUser;
next();
} catch {
return res.status(401).json({ message: "Invalid or expired token." });
34}
}
export function authorize(roles: Role[]) {
return (req: Request, res: Response, next: NextFunction) => {
if (!req.user || !roles.includes(req.user.role)) {
return res.status(403).json({ message: "You are not allowed to perform this action." });
}
next();
};
}

Implementation is the stage where the approved system design is converted into a working
software solution. For the Northern Health Link Software, implementation was planned
around a mobile-first front end, a secure back end, and a structured PostgreSQL database.

improved before final integration.
The implementation followed the functional requirements of the system, including user
login, role-based access control, patient registration, appointment scheduling, video
consultation support, lab request management, result upload, prescription management,
medication delivery tracking, notifications, reporting, and administration.

8.4 Implementation Summary
The implementation of Northern Health Link Software shows how the system design can be
translated into a working digital healthcare platform. The main implementation focus was to
provide secure access, reliable patient record management, remote consultation support, lab
and prescription handling, and medication delivery tracking.
Although challenges such as low connectivity, privacy protection, role control, and module
integration were encountered, the chosen implementation approach helped reduce
complexity. The lessons learnt from the process can guide future improvements such as
offline synchronization, SMS alerts, stronger analytics, and wider deployment across more
rural districts.
53FUTURE PLAN
549. Future Plan
The Northern Health Link Software will continue to evolve to improve healthcare delivery
in rural communities across Ghana. Future enhancements will include the integration of
Artificial Intelligence (AI) for preliminary disease risk assessment and clinical decision
support, offline functionality for areas with poor internet connectivity, and support for
additional regional languages to improve accessibility. The system also aims to integrate
with national health information systems and electronic medical records to facilitate
seamless data sharing. Furthermore, expansion to more districts, inclusion of telepharmacy
services, and the use of data analytics for public health surveillance and healthcare planning
are envisioned to increase the platform's impact and sustainability.
Summary: Future development will focus on AI-assisted healthcare, offline access, national
system integration, expanded geographical coverage, multilingual support, and advanced
analytics to strengthen healthcare delivery in underserved communities.
